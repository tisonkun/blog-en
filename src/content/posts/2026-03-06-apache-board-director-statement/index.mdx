I am Zili Chen, often known as @tison.

I've been a member of the Apache Software Foundation for four years. And I've served the board for the past year. It is my honor to accept the nomination to be a Board member this term.

You can read my [last year's position statement](/posts/apache-board-director-statement) for a brief introduction to my ASF story.

I have three primary goals if elected to the Board for the following term.

## Bring a perspective of the East to the Foundation.

This aligns with my goals from last year. I help podlings with a strong Chinese background evolve into a global shape. You can look at the history of Apache Kvrocks, [Apache OpenDAL](/posts/apache-opendal-graduation), etc. Not only do I guide these projects on how the ASF way works, but they also bring good insights for operating communities: more (CI/CD) automation and great transparency (e.g., new discussion channels). When connecting with other ASF projects, like [Apache DataSketches](https://github.com/apache/datasketches-rust), I can use these practices to improve community building.

## Foster technical innovations within the foundation to catch up with the new waves.

Our foundation has caught the wave several times: Apache Web Server, the Java Ecosystem, Apache Hadoop/Spark (Big Data). This is one of the major reasons why so many open-source producers choose the ASF as the umbrella to host their projects: to stay on the side of the trendsetters.

The ASF way is our treasure and defines who we are. But most projects and developers need an easy-to-follow reason to start contributing to us.

I'm more than glad to see that we're on the way to catching waves:

1. The ASF has more and more Rust projects that become the de facto solution in the Rust ecosystem.
2. The ASF embraces the Agentic Coding trend and actively develops guidelines and produces practices for living in the new world.

I'll continuously observe and participate in these trends, and shape the Foundation to work well with them (primarily, release velocity and software reliability).

## Advocate the ASF way with examples to activate projects and engage contributors.

Our Foundation has many projects that dedicate years to niche domains. In the past year, I learned that some of them are fine living in the domarot status, while others are looking forward to new engagement.

I successfully helped the [Apache Libcloud](https://github.com/apache/libcloud) project find new volunteers to cut a new release and resume the board reporting timeline. It heals the original complaints that the board is only active when pushing projects to the attic rather than helping them based on their situation.

During this experience, I noticed that:

1. We need to share more stories about ASF projects under different statuses. Many PMC members don't know what may happen when the project meets concrete challenges.
2. It's super helpful if a board director (or experienced ASF members) guides projects at risk in a closely collaborative way, when the projects still have good audiences to keep it running, but don't know how to achieve it: many people just wait for a nudge to contribute.

I'll set more examples on how to activate projects and engage contributors, and formalize them as stories or documents as many as possible (this should be with ComDev). In this way, I'm sure we will spot more issues that give the ASF the impression of being bureaucratic and fix them.

Last but not least, I have discussed concrete process improvements with some board directors: about Project Reports, Release Process, and the Incubator. Given our volunteer nature, we may need a few more time to kick them off one by one.

Welcome to talk to me on these topics if you're interested :D
